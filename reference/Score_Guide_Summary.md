# Summary: PTE Academic Test Taker Score Guide (Pearson, 2025)

A summary in our own words of the facts in Pearson's *PTE Academic Test Taker Score Guide* (© Pearson Education Ltd 2025): scoring rules, trait maximums and structure. The guide itself is Pearson's copyrighted publication and is not reproduced here; get the original from Pearson's PTE website. **This is the governing source for how points are awarded.**

## The scores reported

- **Overall score, 10–90.** Built from performance on every question in the test. It is explicitly *not* an average of the four skill scores.
- **Four communicative skill scores, 10–90:** Listening, Reading, Speaking, Writing.
- **Integrated questions** assess two skills at once, and their marks count toward both. The pairings are Listening + Speaking, Reading + Writing, Listening + Writing, and Listening + Reading.
- A test contains **65–75 questions** across **22 question types**, and lasts up to about 2 hours 15 minutes.
- **PTE Academic UKVI** (for UK visas) has the same content and score report, plus a SELT reference number.

## How marking works

- All question types are scored by AI trained on human ratings. Humans also step in for unusual responses the machine can't score reliably, such as very quiet or unclear audio.
- **Human review of Content** is added for Describe Image, Re-tell Lecture, Respond to a Situation, Summarize Group Discussion, Summarize Written Text, Write Essay and Summarize Spoken Text. Write Essay also gets human review of Development/Structure/Coherence and General Linguistic Range. If the AI and the human reviewer disagree, a second human decides.
- **Two kinds of scoring:**
  - **Correct/incorrect:** full points or none.
  - **Partial credit:** points for partly correct responses.
- **Negative marking** applies to both Multiple Choice, Multiple Answers types and to Highlight Incorrect Words. Each wrong selection costs a point, but a question's score never goes below zero.
- **Zero rule:** a response that scores 0 on Content or on Form earns no points at all. Examples: an off-topic essay, an essay under 120 words, or memorised material that doesn't address the prompt.
- **Order of scoring for spoken tasks:** Content is judged first. Only if Content is above 0 are Pronunciation and Oral Fluency scored. Pronunciation and Oral Fluency are AI-scored only.
- **Pronunciation and Oral Fluency** are each scored 0–5, on named levels from *highly proficient* (5) down to non-English or disfluent (0).

## What the Content bands reward

Paraphrased from the band descriptions, as guidance for markers:

- **Spoken retelling and summary tasks** (Re-tell Lecture, Summarize Group Discussion). The top bands need all the main ideas paraphrased in the candidate's own words, with specific detail and clear links between points. Middle bands capture some main ideas but miss detail or connections. Low bands are inaccurate, fragmentary or off the point.
- **Describe Image.** The top bands describe every key feature accurately and interpret how the features relate. Lower bands list only some features or describe them inaccurately.
- **Summarize Written Text and Summarize Spoken Text.** The top band captures every main idea concisely and synthesises it in the writer's own words, with extraneous detail removed. Middle bands lean on copied excerpts or miss some key ideas. The bottom bands are disconnected excerpts or too little to show understanding.
- **Write Essay.** Top bands address every part of the prompt in depth, with a clear, well-developed argument, logical paragraphing and varied, precise language.

## Using scores

Institutions may set an entry requirement on the overall score alone, or on the overall score plus a minimum for some or all of the four communicative skills.
