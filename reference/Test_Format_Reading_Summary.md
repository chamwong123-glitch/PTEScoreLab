# Summary: PTE Academic test format — Part 2, Reading

A summary in our own words of Pearson's official test-format page for Part 2:
<https://www.pearsonpte.com/pte-academic/test-format/reading/> (read 19 September 2026).

Facts only. Pearson's text is not reproduced. For marking detail, [Score_Guide_Summary.md](Score_Guide_Summary.md) governs.

## The part at a glance

- **23–30 minutes**, 5 question types. The page gives no per-question time limit.
- **Every question is reading only.** None of them scores another skill.
- **Chosen options are highlighted on screen,** and a choice can be clicked again to deselect it.

## Question types

| # | Question type | Prompt | What you do | Scoring |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fill in the Blanks (Dropdown) | passage up to 300 words | choose a word for each gap from its dropdown | partial credit, by blank |
| 2 | Multiple Choice, Multiple Answers | passage up to 350 words | choose every correct option — more than one is right | marks for correct options, marks lost for incorrect ones |
| 3 | Reorder Paragraph | text boxes up to 150 words in total, shuffled | put the boxes back in the original order, by dragging or with arrow buttons | partial credit |
| 4 | Fill in the Blanks (Drag and Drop) | passage up to 80 words | drag words from a bank into the gaps; the bank has more words than gaps | partial credit, by blank |
| 5 | Multiple Choice, Single Answer | passage up to 300 words | choose the one correct option | correct or incorrect |

The page doesn't say how many blanks a passage has, or how many options a question offers.

## Pearson's tips, paraphrased

- **Fill in the Blanks (Dropdown):** skim for the overall meaning first. Read what comes before and after each gap, and pick the word that fits both the grammar and the meaning.
- **Multiple Choice, Multiple Answers:** remember that wrong picks cost marks. Notice words that appear in both the options and the passage, and check them in context.
- **Reorder Paragraph:** read every box quickly before moving any. Note the key words, and find the topic sentence first — it usually doesn't begin with a linking word or a pronoun.
- **Fill in the Blanks (Drag and Drop):** think about which words naturally go together, and what part of speech each gap needs. Rule out words that don't fit grammatically.
- **Multiple Choice, Single Answer:** note the key words in the question before reading. Weigh the options, and if you're unsure, pick the most likely one — a wrong answer costs nothing.
