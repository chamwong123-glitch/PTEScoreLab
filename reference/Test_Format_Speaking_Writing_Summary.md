# Summary: PTE Academic test format — Part 1, Speaking & Writing

A summary in our own words of Pearson's official test-format page for Part 1:
<https://www.pearsonpte.com/pte-academic/test-format/speaking-writing/> (read 19 September 2026; the page shows no last-updated date).

Facts only. Pearson's text is not reproduced. For marking detail, [Score_Guide_Summary.md](Score_Guide_Summary.md) governs.

## The part at a glance

- **76–84 minutes**, 9 question types plus an unscored Personal Introduction.
- **Every spoken response is recorded once.** There's no second attempt.
- **Before each spoken response**, a countdown runs until the microphone opens and a short tone signals the start. Answer Short Question is the exception: it has no tone. The status then shows *Recording* and, at the end, *Completed*.
- **Audio prompts play automatically, and only once.**
- **For the listening-based speaking tasks**, notes can be taken on an erasable noteboard while the audio plays.
- **Pronunciation isn't judged against one accent.** Regional varieties of English are accepted where they're easy to understand.
- **The writing tasks** show a live word count, with cut, copy and paste available.

## Question types

| # | Question type | Prompt | Prep | Response | Skills | Scored on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| — | Personal Introduction | written prompt | 25 s | 30 s | not scored | not scored |
| 1 | Read Aloud | text up to 60 words | 30–40 s | varies with text length | Speaking | Content, Pronunciation, Oral Fluency |
| 2 | Repeat Sentence | audio 3–9 s | none (mic opens when audio ends) | 15 s | Listening, Speaking | Content, Pronunciation, Oral Fluency |
| 3 | Describe Image | image | 25 s | 40 s | Speaking | Content, Pronunciation, Oral Fluency |
| 4 | Re-tell Lecture | audio up to 90 s, sometimes with an image | 10 s after audio | 40 s | Listening, Speaking | Content, Pronunciation, Oral Fluency |
| 5 | Answer Short Question | audio 3–9 s | none, no tone | 10 s; stops after 3 s of silence | Listening | correct or incorrect |
| 6 | Summarize Group Discussion | audio up to 3 min, 3 speakers | 10 s after audio | 2 min | Listening, Speaking | Content, Pronunciation, Oral Fluency |
| 7 | Respond to a Situation | situation heard and read, up to 60 words | 10 s | 40 s | Speaking | Content, Pronunciation, Oral Fluency |
| 8 | Summarize Written Text | passage up to 300 words | — | 10 min; one sentence, 5–75 words | Reading, Writing | Content, Form, Grammar, Vocabulary |
| 9 | Write Essay | topic of 2–3 sentences | — | 20 min; 200–300 words | Writing | Content, Development/Structure/Coherence, Form, General Linguistic Range, Grammar, Vocabulary Range, Spelling |

Every scored question type except Answer Short Question uses partial credit.

**Word limits that zero a response:**

- **Summarize Written Text:** under 5 or over 75 words.
- **Write Essay:** under 120 or over 380 words.

## Pearson's tips, paraphrased

- **Read Aloud:** let punctuation guide your phrasing, and stress the important words.
- **Repeat Sentence:** listen for how the sentence is grouped into phrases, and copy its stress and intonation.
- **Describe Image:** concentrate on the main information. Work from what the image shows overall, to the key point, to supporting details, then finish with a conclusion.
- **Re-tell Lecture:** if an image appears, use it to predict the topic and its vocabulary. Speak for the whole 40 seconds without long pauses.
- **Answer Short Question:** answer straight away — within 3 seconds, or the recording stops. Keep the answer short.
- **Summarize Group Discussion:** use the full 2 minutes and cover every main point. Keep talking rather than stopping to fix mistakes.
- **Respond to a Situation:** use the preparation time to pick out the main points and decide whether the situation calls for formal or informal language. Then speak for the full 40 seconds.
- **Summarize Written Text:** build one compound or complex sentence, and check both the word count and that it really is a single sentence.
- **Write Essay:** analyse the prompt for its topic and key words, and notice whether it asks you to agree or disagree.
