# Summary: The Official Guide to PTE Academic (Pearson, 2012 edition)

A summary in our own words of the facts in Pearson's *The Official Guide to PTE Academic* (© Pearson Education Ltd; first published 2010, this edition 2012). The book is Pearson's copyrighted publication and is not reproduced here.

> **Out of date for format and timing.** This edition describes the test as it was in 2012: about three hours long, with up to two essays and two to three spoken summaries, and with "enabling skills" scores. PTE Academic has since been shortened to about two hours, and Summarize Group Discussion and Respond to a Situation have been added. **Wherever this summary conflicts with [Score_Guide_Summary.md](Score_Guide_Summary.md) or [PTE_Academic_Timing_Format_Reference.md](PTE_Academic_Timing_Format_Reference.md), those govern.** This book is kept as a reference for how the questions behave on screen, and for preparation strategy.

## The 2012 test, for context

| | 2012 (this book) | Today |
|---|---|---|
| Length | ~3 hours, plus an optional 10-minute break | up to ~2 h 15 min |
| Part 1 Speaking & Writing | 77–93 min; 1–2 essays; 2–3 Summarize Written Text | 76–84 min; 1 essay; 2 Summarize Written Text; adds Summarize Group Discussion and Respond to a Situation |
| Part 2 Reading | 32–41 min, 15–20 items | 23–30 min |
| Part 3 Listening | 45–57 min; 2–3 Summarize Spoken Text | 31–39 min; 1 Summarize Spoken Text |
| Scores reported | overall, communicative skills, **enabling skills** | overall and communicative skills only |

The six 2012 enabling skills were grammar, oral fluency, pronunciation, spelling, vocabulary and written discourse. They survive as marking traits inside individual question types, but are no longer reported as separate scores.

## On-screen behaviour (still useful)

- **Recordings play once**, and a status box counts down before audio starts: about 3 s for Repeat Sentence, 5–7 s for most listening items, 10 s for Highlight Correct Summary and Highlight Incorrect Words, and 12 s for Summarize Spoken Text.
- **Spoken responses** begin after a short tone. The recording status moves through *Recording* to *Completed*, and a response can't be re-recorded.
- **Personal Introduction** is 25 s to read the prompt and 30 s to record. It isn't scored, but it is sent to institutions along with the score report.
- **Candidates take notes** on an erasable noteboard booklet.
- **Writing tasks** have an on-screen word count and a countdown timer. When time runs out, the test moves on automatically.

## Question types: what the 2012 book says, and how it compares with today

| Question type | 2012 book | Compared with today |
|---|---|---|
| Read Aloud | Text up to 60 words; 30–40 s to prepare (varies with length); 40 s to read. Content is counted word by word. | Same |
| Repeat Sentence | 15 s to respond. Content counts correct word sequences. | Same |
| Describe Image | 25 s to study, 40 s to speak | Same |
| Re-tell Lecture | Audio 60–90 s; 10 s to prepare, 40 s to speak | Today: audio up to 90 s |
| Answer Short Question | 10 s; scored right or wrong on word choice | Same |
| Summarize Written Text | Passage up to 300 words; 10 min; one sentence of 5–75 words, otherwise no credit | Same |
| Write Essay | 20 min; 200–300 words; below 120 or above 380 earns no credit | Same (one essay today, not one or two) |
| Multiple Choice, Single Answer (reading) | Text up to 300 words | Same |
| Multiple Choice, Multiple Answers (reading) | Text up to 300 words; −1 per wrong option | Today: text up to 350 words |
| Reorder Paragraph | Text up to 150 words; partial credit | Same |
| Fill in the Blanks (Drag and Drop) | Text up to 80 words; 4–5 per test | Same |
| Fill in the Blanks (Dropdown) | Text up to 300 words; up to 6 blanks, each with up to 5 choices | Same |
| Summarize Spoken Text | Audio 60–90 s; 10 min; 50–70 words, and below 40 or above 100 earns no credit | Same (one per test today, not two or three) |
| Multiple Choice, Multiple Answers (listening) | Audio 40–90 s; −1 per wrong option | Today: audio 80–120 s |

| Highlight Incorrect Words | Audio 15–50 s; up to 7 deliberate errors; −1 per wrong click | Same |
| Write from Dictation | Each word must be right and correctly spelled | Today: audio 3–5 s |

## Preparation strategies (paraphrased)

- **Read Aloud.** Use the preparation time to break the text into meaningful chunks. Speak at a natural pace, and keep going after a slip rather than restarting.
- **Repeat Sentence.** Listen for the whole sentence rather than decoding it word by word. Start speaking promptly, and keep the speaker's rhythm.
- **Describe Image and Re-tell Lecture.** Open with the main point or the overall picture, then give specific details: figures, names, examples. Close with a short conclusion.
- **Summarize Written Text.** Find the main idea and the key supporting points, then combine them into one grammatical sentence with connecting words.
- **Write Essay.** Plan before writing. Take a clear position, give reasons with examples, and keep to the 200–300 word range. Leave time to check grammar and spelling.
- **Multiple choice.** Read the question first. Only choose options the text clearly supports, because wrong choices cost marks on the multiple-answer types.
- **Reorder Paragraph.** The opening sentence usually states the topic, and has no pronouns or linking words pointing back to anything earlier. Chain the rest using pronouns, articles, linking words and chronology.
- **Fill in the blanks.** Use grammar (the part of speech needed) and collocation, not just meaning. Read the whole sentence before choosing.
- **Listening.** Skim the prompt and the options before the audio starts, and note key words while listening. For Highlight Incorrect Words, follow the text at the speaker's pace.
- **Write from Dictation.** Write down as many words as you catch. Each correct word scores, so check spelling and plurals.
